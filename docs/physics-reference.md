# Real-world stone skipping — reference values

For auditing the sim against reality. Not design direction; just the ground truth to check against.

## Angles

- **~20°** — optimal attack angle between stone and water surface. Minimizes required velocity and collision time across a wide range of conditions.
- **~45°** — hard ceiling. No rebound is possible above this incidence angle, regardless of speed or spin.

That pair is the useful part: there's an optimum *and* a cliff.

## Spin

- Gyroscopic stabilization is what keeps the stone's attitude through contact — the skipper imparts angular momentum much larger than the collisional impulse.
- Lab measurements: **tens of rotations per second**; one experiment used Ω = 65 s⁻¹ (~3,900 rpm).
- Without sufficient spin the stone tumbles and the attack angle goes uncontrolled → plonk.

## Speed

- Minimum translational speed is on the order of **a few m/s**. Record attempts are far higher.

## Stone geometry (lab disc)

- Radius 2.5 cm, thickness 2.75 mm, density ratio ρs/ρw = 2.7 (aluminium).
- One recorded collision sequence lasted **32 ms**.

## Lift mechanism

- Surface tension is negligible. The stone acts as a **planing wing** — lift from angled surface + horizontal speed, like a planing hull or a frisbee.

## Competition context

- World Stone Skimming Championships, Easdale Island, Scotland — running since 1997; scored on **distance**, not skips.
- Skipping (count bounces) and skimming (measure distance) are treated as separate disciplines in the real sport. Worth knowing if you split leaderboards.
