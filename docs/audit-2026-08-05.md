# Project audit — 2026-08-05

Full-repo audit: cross-lab cohesion (how hard is the eventual wiring), game
performance (against `10-performance.md` / `11-art-direction.md`), and the
skip-count investigation. Findings are report-only — nothing here was changed,
and `stone-skipping-physics/` had separate work in flight while this ran.

**The one-paragraph verdict:** every lab is individually well-engineered,
heavily commented, and none of it is bad code. But they were built as five
independent *applications*, not five *modules* — and four of them each
independently reached for the most capable version of a shared idea (its own
depth pass, its own post chain, its own render-resolution policy, its own copy
of shared constants) without a shared budget or a shared layer enforcing the
docs. The wiring is not sisyphean *yet*; it becomes so if the items in §A ship
any deeper.

---

## A. Integration blockers — settle these before wiring anything

### A1 · Two Babylon majors + an unpinned CDN third
- sand-sim is on `@babylonjs/core` **9.18** ([sand-sim/package.json](../sand-sim/package.json)); hub, forge, sift are on **8.56**; babylon-water and the physics demo load an **unpinned "latest"** UMD from `cdn.babylonjs.com` ([babylon-water/index.html:75](../babylon-water/index.html)).
- A Babylon-9 `Scene` cannot host Babylon-8 meshes/materials/post-processes (`instanceof`, ShaderStore, plugin API all differ). The crouch-to-sift LOD swap (docs/09) requires the beach and the bed **in one scene** — so one side must migrate.
- **Call to make:** one version at the repo root (9.x is the cheaper direction — sand-sim's WGSL doesn't port backwards), hoisted once, per-lab copies deleted. Convert babylon-water to a Vite lab (it has zero other deps) or at minimum pin the CDN.
- Also standardise on **piecemeal imports** (sand-sim's style) — it's the only style that keeps the throw-time chunk small — and collect sand-sim's two side-effect imports into one documented `babylonSetup.js`.

### A2 · rock-sift runs the world at 4× scale
- `U = 4` ([rock-sift/src/config.js:6](../rock-sift/src/config.js)), gravity −39.24, **54 `* U` call sites across 10 files**. A forge rock (metres) dropped into the sift bed is 4× too small; the SI-only solver in one scene with Havok-at-4× gets quarter-speed falls; sift's SSAO radius depends on the lab's private constant.
- **Call to make:** 1 unit = 1 metre everywhere and delete `U` (Havok's collision-margin worry is solvable with its own world-scale settings) — or explicitly isolate sifting as its own `Scene` with a documented `toWorld()/toMetres()` boundary. Either is fine; implicit 4× is not.

### A3 · The pond is 24 m; the throws need 60–115 m
- The water sim self-documents a **24 m ceiling** at current cell size ([babylon-water/index.html:124](../babylon-water/index.html)); the solver's `game` profile runs ~61 m, `arcade` ~115 m; docs/01 wants ~200 m and marks it "pending feasibility check". A champion throw leaves the pond after ~5 of 28 skips, and every splash past ±12 m is silently dropped.
- The answer is already written in the water file's own comments: the **windowed sim** — a 24 m interaction window following the stone over an analytic ambient surface of arbitrary size. This *is* the docs/01 feasibility check. Highest-leverage water task there is.
- Related: the solver's `water(x,z,t)` hook and the water sim have **no meeting point** — heights live only in a GPU texture; there is no JS `sampleHeight`. Port the analytic `AMBIENT_GLSL` to a JS twin (sand-sim's `beachParams.js` already demonstrates the "JS twin of the bake" pattern) and the stone stops skipping off an invisible flat plane.

### A4 · babylon-water is unimportable; no lab can be unmounted
- The water page is 1,876 lines of top-level script — no function to call, no module, knobs as `window.*` globals. It is the single most load-bearing visual and the only lab with **no seam at all**. Extract `createWater(scene, camera, engine) → { stoneImpact, sampleHeight, setWind, update, dispose }` (do it together with the windowed sim — same code).
- **No lab has a `dispose()`**; sift and forge add window listeners they never remove. The hub mounts one screen at a time — every revisit would leak an engine and a render loop. Standardise the boot contract on sand-sim's shape, extended: `mount({ scene, camera, engine, opts }) → { update(dt), dispose() }`. Labs must take a **scene**, not a canvas, or the beach and the bed can never share a frame.
- Three post-process stacks fight for the camera (water's DefaultRenderingPipeline, sift's imageProcessing+SSAO2, sand's custom WGSL chain), and the "scribble attaches after tonemap" ordering rule is enforced by comment only. One post-chain owner; labs contribute passes, not pipelines.

### A5 · Three rock formats, no bridge
- rock-forge: radius-function on a shared icosphere (+ hull points + per-archetype densities 2350–2900). rock-sift: scanned GLBs (flat 2650). Solver: analytic disc `{radius, thickness, aspect, edgeRoundness, roughness, comOffset}` (2700). **Nothing converts between them** — docs/04's stone table is still TBD, and today the sifted rock and the skipped rock are unrelated objects.
- **The path:** make forge's radius function canonical — it already yields render (shape texture), rigid body (`buildHullPoints`), and, with ~40 lines of `forge/solverParams.js` (radius/thickness/aspect/mass/comOffset from existing metrics), the solver's stone. Then re-fit the scans as forge archetypes. Also: `skipRating()` is duplicated verbatim in sift and forge — move to `shared/rockRating.js` and derive its constants from the solver's optimum instead of hand-tuning twice.
- Open design decision docs/02 must make: procedural (forge's argument) vs scanned (sift deleted its generator as "blobs"). Decide in engine — forge has a side-by-side reference toggle for exactly this.

### A6 · Determinism: two real breaks, four cosmetic ones
- The solver is the model citizen: zero `Math.random`, fixed-tick `advance()`, `replayable` flag, `checksum()`.
- **Breaks the daily promise today:** `fetchBakedBed(manifestUrl, pick = Math.random())` ([rock-sift/src/bed.js:260](../rock-sift/src/bed.js)) and the bed reshuffle ([shore.js:151](../rock-sift/src/shore.js)) — same seed, different beds. Fix by requiring the pick argument (a defaulted `Math.random()` is the worst kind of break) and threading the daily seed.
- Water/sand particles use `Math.random` freely (spray, foam, dig plumes, footsteps). The ripple *height field* is deterministic — only cosmetics are random — but anything that ever feeds the skip counter must be seeded.
- `mulberry32` exists in three copies; only forge has `hash32` for sub-streams. Promote to `shared/rng.js`, seed the scribble noise lattices (5 copies, all unseeded — share cards won't be pixel-reproducible), and add a `Math.random` lint check to `npm test`. docs/04 said it: cheap now, painful to retrofit.

### A7 · One sky, four grades
- All four 3D labs load the same HDRI. Then: water/forge ACES @ 1.0 with warmth 28/30/6 (matching); sift ACES @ **1.05, contrast 1.15, zero warmth**; sand-sim **AgX @ 0.35**. And forge's directional sun is **mirrored in X/Z** against the sky the other labs agree on ([rock-forge/src/babylon/scene.js:83](../rock-forge/src/babylon/scene.js)) — forge rocks carry highlights on the opposite face from sift rocks under the same sky.
- sand-sim currently honours only the pastel half of the shared dials — the look keys (exposure/warmth/hue/saturation) are silently dropped, so dialling warmth on the water page does nothing on the beach.
- **Fix:** derive the sun from the HDRI once (sand-sim's `_findSun` is the only drift-proof approach) and export from `shared/sky.js`; one grade `{tonemap, exposure, contrast, warmth…}` in `shared/grade.js`; map sand-sim's tonemap uniforms onto the look keys.

## B. Performance — against the docs/10 contract

Floor: Iris Plus G7, 60fps hard during the throw, fixed quality, sand pausable,
WebGL fallback first-class, **resolution is the biggest lever**. docs/11: keep
the aliasing (no MSAA/TAA), avoid SSR.

### B1 · The biggest lever is set to its worst value in two labs
- **babylon-water renders at devicePixelRatio × supersample** — 2× on Retina = ~5.2 Mpx instead of 1.3; everything downstream paid 4×, ~130 MB of render-target VRAM is a direct function of it ([index.html:481](../babylon-water/index.html)).
- **rock-sift passes `adaptToDeviceRatio: true`** ([main.js:50](../rock-sift/src/main.js)) — same 4×, on a page that rasterises the scene **four times** (shadow map, SSAO2 prepass MRT, beauty, scribble's own depth pass) then runs ~6 full-res fullscreen passes.
- rock-forge and sand-sim get this right (1× / `1/resolutionScale`); sand-sim's WebGPU app is the reference. **Fix is one line in each lab.**
- Both labs also request `antialias: true` (docs/11 says the aliasing is the look).

### B2 · sand-sim's post chain fights the art direction
- TAA is **on by default** — full-res pass + two RGBA16F history buffers + per-frame projection jitter — to remove exactly the aliasing docs/11 keeps. SSR is compiled into the chain (off by default, but "passes never detach" means it still runs as a full-screen copy). Default preset is "ultra".
- Terrain clipmap: **~333k triangles rendered 5×/frame** (3 shadow cascades + depth prepass + beauty), never culled; outer two clipmap rings reach ~1 km against a 512 m world texture — data-free triangles. Cascades are 3×2048² (~100 MB).
- Total WebGPU-path VRAM ≈ **290 MB @1080p** — a lot for an integrated GPU sharing 8 GB.
- The deformation gate (`S.simGate`) **genuinely gates** (returns before render, banks owed relaxation — the reference implementation of docs/10 pausability). Caveats: one footstep keeps it awake 4 s, and there's no external `freeze()` for the throw. The frame loop is raw-delta, not fixed-tick — fine today, flag before footprint/stance becomes score-bearing.

### B3 · The scribble tax: a full scene depth pass per lab, to mask the sky
- `shared/scribble-fx.js` calls `scene.enableDepthRenderer` — an entire extra full-res scene render whose only surviving consumer is one `step()` for the sky mask. In rock-sift this **duplicates the SSAO2 prepass depth that already exists**; in rock-forge the depth is geometrically wrong anyway (undisplaced spheres — the reason outlines were removed). sand-sim's WGSL pass reuses its own prepass — the correct pattern.
- **Fix once in shared:** accept an optional `depthTexture`; feed sift's prepass; for labs with none, mask sky via stencil/skybox-alpha sentinel instead of a whole pass.

### B4 · The water sim never idles
- `stepSimulation()` + `updateNormals()` run unconditionally — 1024² RGBA32F ping-pong ≈ 64 MB traffic/frame at rest, and **each drop is its own full-screen pass** (up to 13/frame ≈ 420 MB during a skip run). No disturbance gate exists (only a manual pause key). Mirror sand-sim's gate; batch drops into one pass with a uniform array.
- Wasted-by-default: scribble's inline copy still does 4 depth taps for `edgeDark` which is 0; the pastel default `warp: 0` similarly skips nothing.

### B5 · The solver allocates ~40k objects in an impact frame — and the adapter has a determinism bug
- The `V`/`Q` vector helpers return fresh literals; the 144-panel loop × ~70 substeps of a fast contact ≈ tens of thousands of allocations **on the exact frames the 60fps-hard window covers**. Converting to out-parameter/flat-array form is the highest-value change in that lab (L effort).
- **One-line, do-first:** `babylonAdapter.js:95` calls `sim.step(dt)` with raw frame delta — the exact frame-rate-dependent path the solver's own docs call "fatal for a leaderboard". The demo uses `advance()` correctly; the reusable adapter must too.
- Demo: CPU Gerstner displacement + `createNormals(true)` over 26k verts every frame; unbounded trail array rebuilt per frame (same pattern in the adapter).

### B6 · Static work re-done every frame; assets over budget
- rock-forge re-renders its **2048² shadow map every frame through the full displacing vertex shader** for a field that never moves (`refreshRate = RENDER_ONCE` + explicit invalidation on rebuild ≈ half its vertex load). Its camera-move rebucket threshold is 2 cm (fires every orbit frame); the 16-float scalar copy should be `TypedArray.set`.
- Texture diet: `rock_027` is the only 2K set — 22 MB download, ~50 MB VRAM, 43% of the rock budget; 1K matches the rest. Decoded rock textures total ~157 MB. The 17 MB 4k HDRI is loaded per lab while a 1.2 MB 1k version sits beside it (labs should load 1k by default). The two Stefan TTFs are **10.75 MB and block the splash** behind `document.fonts.ready` — convert to WOFF2 (and consider a digits-subset cut for the score font, per docs/06 "scores only").
- rock-sift: ground is 80k triangles × 4 passes for a 2 cm dune displacement (`subdivisions 200 → 32`); Havok steps 2×/frame over 540 sleeping hulls with no disturbance gate (`PHYSICS_SUBSTEP_MS 1/120 → 1/60` halves it — re-run sift-test to validate); `getMassProperties()` allocates per substep while carrying.

### B7 · Already correct — preserve these
- sand-sim's `deformation.js` (pausability + zero per-frame allocation) is the standard the rest should be held to.
- rock-sift's baked-bed path (3.4 s pour → 28 ms restore) and `examine.prewarm` (compiles behind the loading screen, dodges the whole-bed recompile trap).
- The solver's determinism layer (fixed tick, replayable flag, checksum).
- rock-forge's engine setup (1× resolution — the only Babylon-8 lab that got it right).

## C. Skip counts — why "good throws" die early in the demo

The solver's honest physics caps at ~13 hops **by design**; the assist knobs
are the intended route to champion counts (`game` ≈ 28, `arcade` ≈ 63). The
demo undermines them three ways:

1. **Stale default:** the demo boots with `attitudeAssist: 6` ([demo/index.html:149](../stone-skipping-physics/demo/index.html)) — the value from *before* the assist was re-targeted at angular momentum and recalibrated to **20** ([stoneSkipping.js's game profile](../stone-skipping-physics/src/stoneSkipping.js)). The env spread `{...assistParams}` then **overrides the selected profile** with that stale 6 — you've been throwing with roughly a third of the intended attitude hold, and runs die of nose-down attitude walk around hop 6–10 with energy to spare.
2. **Unreachable ceiling:** the Attitude-hold slider maxes at **16** — below game's 20 and arcade's 32 — and the slider sync **clamps stored values into slider range**, so even picking "arcade" in the dropdown gets squashed to 16.
3. **Spin scaling:** assist scales by spin ÷ 40 rev/s — low-spin throws weaken it further (the `casual` preset at 12 rev/s gets ~30% of nominal assist).

Fix is three lines in the demo (defaults → the game profile's values; slider
max ≥ 32; don't clamp profile-applied values). Left unapplied here — separate
solver work was in flight in that folder during this audit.
